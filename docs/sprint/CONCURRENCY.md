# CONCURRENCY: Layer 1 results (no model; the browser on its own)

Plan and bars (fixed before any row): `CONCURRENCY-PLAN.md`. Data: `conc2/layer1-grid.tsv` (+ `.infra`), commit 749d97ef, chrome_ver=145.0.7632.116,
harness `bench/conc/harness.py`, tables generated by `bench/conc/analyze.py` (gate: `tests/sprint/test_conc2_analysis.sh`).
3 runs per (arm, N) cell, arms interleaved, each cell in its own 8 GB cgroup (`MemoryMax=8G`, swap 0), run only while MemAvailable >= 20 GB.
The host was shared with a running judged benchmark worker the whole time (host load average ranged roughly 8 to 35; recorded per row as `host_load1`).
Traces: 8 per agent (5 local own-suite, 3 frozen real pages rfc/w3c/mdn); capacity is judged on the real-page traces as registered.

## Verdicts, as pre-registered (read these first)

- **Cost win (peak PSS per successful task at N=50, >=30% lower than best Chrome arm): NOT EVALUABLE.** In the 8 GB cage every Chrome arm
  (chrome-mcp, pw-proc, pw-shared) was OOM-killed at N=50 in all 3 runs (24 died cells in total, matched by 24 `oom-kill` scope failures in the
  journal), so there is no Chrome N=50 number to compare with. It is not scored as a pass. The nearest comparable point, N=25 against pw-shared
  (the only Chrome arm that survived N=25), is reported as exploratory below, not as the registered test.
- **Capacity win (>=1.5x best Chrome arm incl. pw-shared, real-page traces): UNRESOLVED for all three engine rows.** On the ladder
  (1,5,10,25,50,100) engine-proc-full and engine-fork keep success within 5 points up to N=50, pw-shared up to N=25, but the true capacities
  lie in [50,99] and [25,49], so the ratio is anywhere from 1.02x to 3.96x. The bisection step has not been run (follow-up CONC.2.a). No win is claimed.
  engine-proc-lean holds only to N=25 (same bracket as pw-shared): no win at ladder resolution.
- **Success within 5 points of best Chrome arm at the same N:** holds at N=1..10 for every arm (all 1.00). At N=25 engine-proc-lean is 0.98 vs
  1.00 (within). At N=50 and 100 there is no surviving Chrome arm to compare with.
- **Against one Chrome per agent** (chrome-mcp, pw-proc; labelled separately as the plan requires): their capacity is 10 (OOM-killed at N=25), the engine
  rows hold to >=25. At N=10 peak PSS is 4.4 GB (chrome-mcp) and 4.8 GB (pw-proc) against 0.9-1.0 GB for the engine rows.
- Engine rows degrade at N=50/100 by timeouts, not memory (p95 per call 25-60 s while PSS is 3.5-7.2 GB; no engine cell was OOM-killed).
  Throughput peaks at N=25 for engine-proc-full (232 traces/min) and flattens or falls above that: the host has finite cores.
- Throughput is NOT a manuk win at small N: pw-proc/pw-shared complete 40 traces/min at N=1 against 22 for engine-proc (p50/p95 per call is also lower on
  Playwright: p95 ~1.0 s vs ~2.6 s at N=1). The comparison here is memory and capacity, not speed.
- Memory per successful task (peak PSS / traces passed): engine rows 10-11 MB at N=25..100; pw-shared 29 MB at N=25 (34 at N=10); chrome-mcp 55 and
  pw-proc 60 MB at N=10. The exploratory N=25 ratio versus pw-shared is 62-64% lower for the engine rows. It is a different N from the registered one and
  pw-shared is the only Chrome arm there.

## Caveats

- Cells that died are OOM kills of the whole cage; they are counted as success 0 for capacity (same rule as `harness.py capacity`) and listed below, never dropped.
- Real-page traces are 3 frozen local snapshots (rfc, w3c, mdn), not the live web. Capacity here is for those; no claim about other sites.
- Ladder resolution only. 3 runs per cell: the bands are min-max, not confidence intervals.
- The host was loaded by another job; arms were interleaved so it hits all arms, but absolute throughput numbers are load-dependent.
- No README number is updated from this file until the unresolved items are settled (README updates are limited to the numbers marked above as measured).
